Analyze competing hypotheses about depression causation:

Hypothesis A (Serotonin Deficiency): Major depressive disorder is primarily caused by low serotonin concentration or reduced serotonin activity.

Hypothesis B (Neuroinflammation / Immune Dysregulation): Major depressive disorder is primarily caused by chronic inflammation, cytokine signaling, or immune dysregulation.

Hypothesis C (Neuroplasticity / BDNF / Stress-Circuit Dysfunction): Major depressive disorder is primarily caused by impaired neuroplasticity, reduced BDNF-related signaling, and stress-related dysfunction in mood circuits.

Questions:
1. Which hypothesis has the strongest causal evidence? Rank by evidence type, not by rhetorical plausibility.
2. Are the three hypotheses mutually exclusive, or do they occupy different causal layers?
3. Which specific evidence items become unreliable, scope-limited, or overgeneralized after cross-examination?
4. Design an experiment that would distinguish causal primacy among A, B, and C.
