Analyze competing hypotheses about the Fermi paradox:

Hypothesis A (Rare Emergence / Great Filter Before Us):
Life, complex life, intelligence, or technological civilization is intrinsically rare because one or more evolutionary or planetary steps are difficult.

Hypothesis B (Short-Lived or Self-Limiting Technological Civilizations):
Technological civilizations may arise, but their detectable or expansion-capable phase is short, unstable, self-limiting, or interrupted before galaxy-scale visibility.

Hypothesis C (Non-Detection / Non-Expansion / Observational Selection):
Technological civilizations may exist, but current searches are incomplete, expansion is not inevitable, civilizations may be quiet, sustainable, spatially patchy, or deliberately non-contacting.

Questions:
1. Which hypothesis has the strongest causal or evidential support? Rank by evidence type, not rhetorical plausibility.
2. Are the three hypotheses mutually exclusive, or do they occupy different causal layers?
3. Which specific evidence items become unreliable, scope-limited, or overgeneralized after cross-examination?
4. Design an experiment, survey program, or observational test that would distinguish causal primacy among A, B, and C.
