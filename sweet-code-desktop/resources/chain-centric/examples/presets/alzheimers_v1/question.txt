Analyze competing hypotheses about Alzheimer's disease causation:

Hypothesis A (Amyloid-Beta Cascade): Amyloid-beta plaque deposition is the primary etiological driver.
- Hardy & Higgins (Science, 1992) -- Amyloid cascade hypothesis.
- Selkoe (Nature Medicine, 2016) -- AB oligomers directly impair synaptic function.
- Note: 2022 Science investigation revealed image manipulation in a key AB*56 paper (Lesne et al., 2006). Multiple AB-targeting trials have failed.

Hypothesis B (Tau Propagation): Tau tangle pathology and trans-synaptic spread is the proximate lethal mechanism.
- Braak staging (Braak & Braak, 1991) -- Tau progression correlates tightly with cognitive decline.
- Clavaguera et al. (Nature Cell Biology, 2009) -- Tau fibril injection triggers seeding and propagation.
- Holmes et al. (PNAS, 2014) -- Tau PET predicts cognitive trajectory better than amyloid PET.

Hypothesis C (Neuroinflammation / Immune Dysfunction): Chronic microglial activation is the primary driver.
- Heneka et al. (Lancet Neurology, 2020) -- GWAS risk variants (TREM2, CD33) converge on microglial function.
- Tweedie et al. (Journal of Neuroinflammation, 2007) -- TNF-alpha overexpression recapitulates AD-like neurodegeneration.
- Lecanemab (anti-AB) showed modest clinical benefit -- consistent with AB as trigger but not sole cause.

Reviews: Long & Holtzman (Cell, 2019), Sala Frigerio & De Strooper (Annual Review Neuroscience, 2016).

1. Which hypothesis has the strongest causal evidence? Rank by evidence type.
2. Are the three hypotheses mutually exclusive?
3. Design an experiment to distinguish causal primacy.
