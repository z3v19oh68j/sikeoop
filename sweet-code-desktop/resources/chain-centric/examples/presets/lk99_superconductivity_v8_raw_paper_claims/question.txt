Analyze competing hypotheses about the LK-99 room-temperature superconductivity controversy:

Hypothesis A (True Superconductivity):
LK-99 or a correctly synthesized Cu-substituted lead apatite phase is a room-temperature, ambient-pressure superconductor.

Hypothesis B (Impurity / Measurement Artifact):
The reported superconducting-like signals are caused by impurities, phase transitions, ferromagnetic/diamagnetic artifacts, sample inhomogeneity, contact artifacts, or measurement interpretation errors rather than bulk superconductivity.

Hypothesis C (Conditional / Non-Bulk / Narrow-Phase Effect):
Some LK-99-like samples may show unusual electronic or magnetic behavior, but any effect is not robust bulk room-temperature superconductivity and depends on narrow composition, defects, interfaces, or synthesis conditions.

Questions:
1. Which hypothesis has the strongest evidential support? Rank by evidence type, not by excitement or plausibility.
2. Are the three hypotheses mutually exclusive, or do they occupy different levels of explanation?
3. Which specific evidence items become unreliable, scope-limited, or overgeneralized after cross-examination?
4. Design a decisive replication or materials-characterization experiment that would distinguish A, B, and C.
